01
Add nuance to the brief

Creative & production approach

  • How would you creatively and technically enhance and write the production brief for this campaign?
  • Mention specifics: shooting style, scale, visual references and brand alignment.

Understanding the problem statement

The core objective is to communicate Meesho’s easy return policy and break the hesitation consumers may have around returning products bought online.

The creative opportunity is to say this in a quirky, entertaining and visually engaging way, so the message reaches a mass audience without feeling like a conventional product advertisement.

Shooting style & visual treatment

The existing campaign opens strongly. The upbeat track is an immediate hook within the first five seconds, and its familiar, energetic rhythm builds curiosity. I would retain it and enhance the visual treatment to match its energy.

For Tier 2–3, the outdoors should feel rooted in everyday life: busier market areas, narrow gullies, mohallas and neighbourhood streets, not wider city-establishing shots. The protagonist’s home already sets the right tone.

The camera becomes more dynamic: handheld movement, tracking shots and occasional POV-style perspectives that follow the characters, so the camera moves with the rhythm of the music.

▶ Visual reference: POV camera movement

Casting & character performances

The characters bring the drama and humour. With a comic undertone, performances need natural facial reactions, comic timing and relatable body language.

Frustrations like chasing a delivery person, or the hassle of returning a product, should land through subtle expressions and situational humour rather than exaggerated acting.

The aim is for the audience to recognise these everyday situations and find the humour in them.

See the casting strategy →

Design & brand alignment

I would revisit the brand identity, particularly the end slate. The current ‘M’ logo, with multiple colours inside the mark, feels disconnected from the rest of the communication.

I would refer to Meesho’s brand guideline document and align the end slate with its approved colour palette, typography, logo treatment and design language.

Having supervised post-production across multiple brands, I treat brand guidelines as part of the production process. Visual consistency shapes how audiences recognise a brand.

Brand positioning & consumer trust

There is an opportunity to strengthen Meesho’s positioning among Tier 1 audiences. I haven’t been a regular Meesho user, and a few people in my circle associated the platform mainly with inexpensive products, with some hesitation about quality and delivery reliability.

These are anecdotal observations, not representative research, but they point to a trust opportunity. Easy returns shouldn’t just signal convenience; they should reassure people that trying something new online carries less hesitation.

The shortlist is built on the brief’s communication objective, storytelling approach and visual language. It goes beyond portfolios to how each director understands consumer behaviour and e-commerce, and how well they turn a brand proposition into a relatable narrative.

For a campaign aimed at Tier 2–3 while staying relevant to Tier 1, the storytelling has to feel rooted in everyday experience, not staged or overly constructed.

Each director brings a distinct strength in natural performances, authentic character behaviour, regional dialects and the cultural details that make a setting believable.

03
Casting strategy

Familiarity over fame

  • What’s your approach to casting talent who connect with both Tier 1 and Tier 2 audiences?
  • Celebrity, character actors, influencers, or a mix, and why? Include 2 sample casting options with rationale.
Characters with the face and personality of “agle ghar ka ladka ya ladki”.Casting philosophy

Someone familiar, approachable, almost a person the audience has met in their own neighbourhood. That instant “I’ve seen someone like this before” is the emotional way in. The audience first connects with the character, then recognises the situation, and finally absorbs the proposition.

The product integration should feel like the natural resolution of the situation, not an interruption of the story.

Brand and marketing view: relatability across demographics without making the film feel limited to one geography or segment. Language, accents, body language and interpersonal dynamics should feel organic, not exaggerated to represent a Tier 2–3 setting.

Production view: strong performers with natural screen presence and comic timing, who complement the director’s treatment and keep the narrative believable.

Casting mix: performance-led, not popularity-led

My preference

A mix led primarily by character actors and fresh, relatable faces, rather than building the campaign around celebrity recognition.

For a proposition-led campaign that breaks the perception that online returns are cumbersome, the strength of the storytelling and the credibility of the characters come before the popularity of the faces. More of the budget then goes to performance, direction, production quality and storytelling, with room to scale across audiences and platforms.

Where celebrity or influencers fit

They bring visibility, reach and familiarity, but also higher talent cost, entourage requirements, scheduling dependencies and production complexity.

Celebrity association makes sense with a specific amplification objective: a festive campaign, a new category launch, a large-scale brand initiative or a significant awareness push. Influencers can be used selectively when their presence serves a defined marketing objective and justifies the investment.

What inspires me

Perfect Family · SonyLIV

Watch on YouTube ↗
  • Mention a piece of content or movie that inspired you, and break down the nuances that worked for the production and why.

Content absorption is a big part of staying relevant in this industry. Inspiration doesn’t only come from actively looking for references. Sometimes it comes from consuming content every day, and that goes beyond Instagram reels or short-form video: a film, a series, a documentary, or something you stumble upon.

One series that stayed with me is Perfect Family on SonyLIV. The Karkaria family is brought together for therapy after a child’s emotional crisis forces them to confront the resentments, insecurities and unresolved issues in their household.

What stood out was the writing of its characters. Each has a distinct personality, perspective, emotional baggage and set of contradictions. Nobody is entirely right or wrong. You gradually understand why they behave the way they do, how upbringing shaped their relationships, and how generational trauma gets passed down without people realising it.

Nuance 01

Character-driven storytelling

No conventional hero or antagonist. Every family member has their own emotional journey, so the characters feel lived-in, familiar and flawed, and viewers recognise people from their own lives.

Nuance 02

Humour and emotion in balance

Despite heavy subjects, it never turns overwhelmingly dramatic. It finds humour in everyday family behaviour, awkward conversations and generational differences, staying relatable without diluting emotional depth.

Nuance 03

Performances and authenticity

The ensemble brings real realism. Conversations, arguments, silences and awkward moments never feel manufactured, which lets the writing breathe and makes the smallest interactions meaningful.

I love the irony of the title. Perfect Family is perhaps the biggest contradiction in the series. Everyone maintains the appearance of a perfect family while years of resentment, insecurity, compromise and unspoken emotion sit underneath. The show strips that illusion away to reveal imperfect people trying to work out how to live with, understand and love one another.

From a production perspective, it reminds me that you don’t always need grand scale, extravagant sets or larger-than-life situations to create impact. Strong writing, layered characters, authentic performances and an honest understanding of human behaviour can be just as powerful.

Good content doesn’t always have to give the audience answers. Sometimes, it just needs to make them ask the right questions.
